# The Pose Problem: Why Art Style Is the Only Real Protection

## The Experiment

I ran an experiment once: take one artist's image and count how many times that
same pose has been redone across the internet.

The result was predictable in hindsight — **the simpler the pose, the higher the
repeat count.** A dynamic, contorted pose is rare. A standing character with one
hand on the hip is everywhere.

We just don't notice it because the images are posted at different times, by
different people, in different communities.

## The Copycat Accusation Problem

This is the same mechanism people use to claim one artist copied another.

And here is the funny part: **even when the accuser is technically right, they
are probably pointing at the wrong artist.** The image they found first is not
the image that came first.

## "I Only Draw Canon Characters" Doesn't Protect You

It feels safer to draw established characters. It isn't.

You have no idea if someone with a similar art style already drew that character
in a similar way, years before you, on a platform you have never visited.

## OCs Don't Save You Either

Original characters feel safer. They aren't, unless you know how to build truly
unique visual features.

Without that discipline, the probability that someone has already made a 90%
identical OC is very high.

## What This Means for AI Training

I believe AI image training works precisely by exploring this empty territory of
the drawing world.

**What does *not* protect you:**

- Watermarks, logos, and visual pollution — they are just noise to be filtered out.

**What actually protects you:**

- A genuinely unique art style.

Anyone who has curated a dataset knows why. A style that is too specific cannot
be tagged, cannot be blended, and cannot be separated from its author — so it
contaminates every caption in the batch. The curator drops it, or the model
overfits and the run is wasted.
